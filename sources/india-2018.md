# Source — India, 2018: child-abduction rumors forwarded on WhatsApp and similar apps, and the mob killings that followed

- **The claim used in the comment:** "about two dozen innocent people killed by mobs" in 2018 after false child-kidnapping rumors spread by forwarded messages.
- **Why "about two dozen" and not a precise number:** counts differ by outlet and by the window counted. Contemporaneous reporting: *The New York Times* (July 2018) described roughly two dozen people killed since April 2018 in rumor-driven attacks; *The Indian Express* tallied around 27 across roughly a year; the Wikipedia compilation lists at least 23 with named cases. A responsible phrasing is "about two dozen." Do not quote a single exact figure as if it were settled.
- **Named cases reported at the time:** a 65-year-old woman and family attacked in Tamil Nadu; five men from a nomadic community killed in Dhule, Maharashtra (1 July 2018); a man killed in Bidar, Karnataka; in Tripura, a man hired by the state to dispel the rumors was himself beaten to death.
- **The causal claim, stated carefully:** the messages did not kill anyone. They created a shared false frame that coordinated groups of people into lethal action against strangers. That is the link the comment relies on, and it is enough for the argument without overclaiming.
- **Platform response:** WhatsApp limited message forwarding in India in mid-2018 and then globally.
- **Where to check:** search the outlets above by name and month (NYT July 2018 "WhatsApp" "lynching"; BBC; The Indian Express; CNN). Prefer the original 2018 reporting to later summaries.
- **Verification status:** the order of magnitude and the named cases are consistent across independent outlets. Whether Marc read the reporting himself is stated in HUMAN.md.
