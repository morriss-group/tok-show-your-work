# 11 — Ten frameworks against the answer (a fresh Grok chat, verbatim) and what it changes

After the comment and the second question were posted, the author asked: if I ran ten philosophical frameworks at this question, would the answer change, or only the perspective? A fresh Grok Heavy chat was given the answer as posted and asked to apply ten named traditions to it, four parts each, with primary citations, and to say at the end whether anything changed beyond vocabulary. Its report is below, unedited. The author's reading follows it.

Verification status: the citations (section numbers, chapter numbers, sutta numbers) are plausible and match how these texts are normally cited, but they were not checked against the texts by the author. Same rule as the rest of this folder: accepted from the model, listed so anyone can check.

---

Clifford's evidentialism
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Moral evidentialism — W. K. Clifford, "The Ethics of Belief" (1877), §I "The Duty of Inquiry."
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes, and exceptionless. Belief and testimony on thin evidence corrupt the believer and poison the common store of knowledge.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Makes the duty absolute. Reach and harm explain why the rule matters; they do not grade it. Affordability is not an exemption.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: Clifford already rejected your membership-fee limit — the rustic in the alehouse has the same duty you want to scale.

James's pragmatic will to believe
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Passional nature may decide a "genuine" option (live, forced, momentous) that intellect cannot settle — William James, "The Will to Believe" (1896), §§I, IV, VIII.
2. ITS ANSWER: Wrong question if one rule covers all shares. Ordinary factual rumor can wait for evidence; a genuine option cannot, and "leave it open" is itself a passional choice.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Dissolves the single scale. Most digital empirical claims are not genuine options; the scale does not govern the cases James defends.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: You already admit verification can bury a true story; James says that is why a verify-first rule is an irrational faith in delay, not just a hardship.

Kantian universalizability
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Act only on maxims you can will as universal law; treat humanity as an end — Immanuel Kant, Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785), 4:421, 4:429; "On a Supposed Right to Lie from Philanthropy" (1797).
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes. A maxim of sharing unchecked claims when convenient cannot be universalized without wrecking testimony, and it uses hearers as mere means.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Makes the core duty categorical (do not pass off the unchecked as known). Degree can remain only as an imperfect duty of care; expected harm does not ground the obligation.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: If how binding the duty is depends on how far the post might travel, you are counting outcomes and you have left Kant.

Consequentialism (Mill, including the harm principle)
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Greatest happiness plus the harm principle — J. S. Mill, Utilitarianism (1861), ch. 2; On Liberty (1859), chs. 1–2 (corn-dealer case).
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes, when an unchecked share is expected to produce more harm than the gains from free circulation. Speech that becomes "a positive instigation to some mischievous act" loses its immunity.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Keeps proportion to reach and harm. Print vs. shouting before a mob is the original scale. Affordability enters as a utility cost.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: Mill also says even false opinions keep true ones from going stale — so a duty that silences people who cannot check shrinks the collision of ideas he wanted.

Aristotelian virtue ethics
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Virtue as a mean judged by practical wisdom — Aristotle, Nicomachean Ethics II.6 (mean); IV.7 (truthfulness; mainly self-presentation — extension to news-checking); VI.5–13 (phronēsis).
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes, as what a truthful person of practical wisdom would do, not as an act-formula. Falsehood is "in itself base."
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Reframes the duty from act-rule to character. Proportionality survives only as what the phronimos sees here, not as reach × harm.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: If virtue is a mean, never posting because the checking is never finished is a vice too — the pedant is not the truthful person.

Care ethics (Gilligan, Noddings)
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Morality as response to particular others in relation — Carol Gilligan, In a Different Voice (1982); Nel Noddings, Caring (1984), ch. 4.
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes, when unverified sharing would neglect or harm people one is related to in care; the duty is to the web, not to a verification principle.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Changes the duty from impersonal reach × harm to relationship. Proximity and vulnerability can outweigh raw virality.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: If I do not know the people a post will hit, care ethics can let distant viral harm off the hook — the opposite of scaling by reach.

Rawls's veil of ignorance
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Principles chosen behind a veil of ignorance — John Rawls, A Theory of Justice (1971), §24 (veil); §11 (two principles); §19 (natural duties). [Extension: Rawls's official subject is the basic structure.]
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes, but first as a rule for institutions. Parties who do not know whether they will be rumor-victim, buried source, or person unable to check would restrain high-harm sharing and reject a verification barrier only the advantaged can clear.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Reframes the duty toward the basic structure (platforms, checkers, access). Reach × harm can survive as maximin precaution; affordability becomes a justice constraint.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: Behind the veil you would protect the person a rumor ruins first, so you do not get a lighter duty just because you guessed the post would not travel.

Scanlon's contractualism
1. THE FRAMEWORK: An act is wrong if any principle permitting it could reasonably be rejected by someone moved to find common terms — T. M. Scanlon, What We Owe to Each Other (1998), ch. 5.
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes. "Share without checking" can be reasonably rejected by those who would bear the costs; "share only if an official checker agrees" can be rejected by those whose true claims would be buried or who cannot pay to verify.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Keeps a scaled shape but changes the metric: justifiability to each affected person, not aggregate reach × harm.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: You scale duty by how far a claim travels, but one person destroyed by a small-circle rumor can reasonably reject your principle even if the post never went viral.

Buddhist right speech
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Right Speech: utter only what is true, beneficial, and timely — Buddha, Majjhima Nikāya 58, Abhayarājakumāra Sutta; four abstentions at SN 45.8.
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes. Unverified speech fails the truth gate; even known-true speech must still be beneficial and timely or it should not be said.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Changes the duty from a scalar of reach × harm to three independent gates. Timing can forbid a verified viral post and permit a quiet true warning.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: A verified high-reach post can still fail "beneficial" or "timely," so your rule treats reach as a reason to speak when the sutta often treats it as a reason not to.

Confucian rectification of names
1. THE FRAMEWORK: Zhengming: if names are not correct, speech will not accord with things; then affairs fail and people do not know how to act — Confucius, Analects 13.3 (cf. 12.11). [Extension from governing speech to individual posting.]
2. ITS ANSWER: Yes. Sharing under a false or inflated name ("news," "fact," "verified") disorders speech and the social world; first put words back onto things.
3. WHAT IT DOES TO MY SCALING RULE: Reframes the duty toward language, roles, and order rather than act-scaling by reach × harm. The anti-official-checker limit becomes the live problem: who is entitled to fix the names.
4. THE SHARPEST OBJECTION: If you refuse every official checker, who online may say a rumor is not "news" — and why isn't that just more of the name-drift 13.3 says starts the collapse?

CONVERGENCE
| Relation to the scaled "yes" | Frameworks |
|---|---|
| Keep a scaled duty | Mill |
| Make it absolute / categorical | Clifford; Kant (core duty: do not pass off the unchecked as known) |
| Dissolve the single rule | James (genuine vs ordinary options) |
| Reframe what the duty is about | Aristotle (character); care (relationship); Rawls (institutions); Scanlon (justifiability to each, not aggregate reach); Buddhist (three gates); Confucian (names/roles) |

WHAT CHANGES: The verdict stays a qualified yes. The justification does not: reach × harm is one consequentialist metric, not the moral fact. Clifford and Kant will not treat affordability or checker-failure as moral limits, only as hardships. Care, Aristotle, Scanlon, and right speech relocate the duty — relation, character, justifiability, three gates. Zhengming makes the anti-official-checker limit the live problem rather than a side constraint. Right speech can forbid the verified viral post the scale treats as most urgent. That is a change in what some acts require, not a change of vocabulary.

## The author's reading

Only one of the ten keeps my rule as I wrote it, and that's Mill, which tells me my "yes, scaled by reach and harm" is a consequentialist's answer wearing Clifford's coat. Three objections land hard enough that I'd have to answer them in class:

1. **Clifford doesn't grant my membership-fee limit.** I cited him for the duty and then excused the people who can't afford it. He didn't. He said the man in the alehouse has the same duty as the scholar. Either I disagree with Clifford on that, and should say so, or my second limit is a hardship, not a moral exemption.
2. **Scanlon's small-circle victim.** I scaled the duty by how far a post travels. But one person ruined by a rumor in a group chat of five can reasonably reject my rule, and the rumor never went anywhere. The moral unit may be the person wronged, not the audience size.
3. **Right speech treats reach as a reason not to speak.** My rule makes a big audience a reason to be careful. The sutta's three gates, true, beneficial, timely, can forbid a fully verified viral post and permit a quiet warning to one person. That inverts my scaling in the cases it was built for.

So: the verdict didn't move, the reasoning did, and three traditions would change what I'm allowed to do in specific cases. That's the difference between an answer and a position, and it's what a class should do to a visitor.
