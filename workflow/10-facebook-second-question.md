A second question for the class, since you've been generous with the first one.

Something changed for me this year. I can now check a claim in about the time it takes to read it. I can pull the original source, the date, the context, and a competing account, and have all of it in front of me before my coffee's cold. Most people can't, or won't. So here's what I keep running into: if I can do that cheaply, do I have a duty to go looking for false claims being shared and put the correct information on those threads? Or is the duty only not to add to the pile myself?

The case for yes: if sharing without checking is wrong because of what it does to other people, then watching a false claim spread, holding the correction, and staying quiet looks like the shipowner's cousin. Some philosophers call this a duty to object. Silence can protect a lie that's already moving.

The case for no: nobody appointed me. There's a good argument that correcting people inside a closed group makes them trust outsiders less, not more, so the correction can strengthen the thing it was aimed at. The people I'd be correcting mostly didn't ask. And a man with better tools showing up to fix everyone's posts is a role that has a name, and it isn't a flattering one.

So which is it? Does having the ability to verify create a duty to correct, or only a duty not to spread? And if it's a duty, who does it stop with, the people I know, the threads I'm already in, or everybody?
